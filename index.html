A next-generation, ready-to-fruit grow block for commercial growers — in development in Colorado.

The problem

Substrate is the wall. The prep line, the clean room, the incubation space, the bags you lose to contamination — together they cap how much you can grow, no matter how much you can sell.

Most growers know the trade already: at some point it becomes worth paying someone else to make the block, just to get the room and the hours back.

What we're building

A ready-to-fruit block that resists contamination on its own — and a reworked way of making it. Prep space becomes growing space, and a bad batch stops taking the month with it.

We're not selling yet. Right now we're talking to growers to make sure we build the right thing.

The homework

This isn't armchair. Behind it is a technical plan running about 470 pages and drawing on more than 500 sources, plus a bench-test campaign running now. Patent pending.

Serious partners can review the technical detail under NDA.

Who's behind it

Rocky Mountain Mushroom Farm is a Colorado company. I came at this from the ground up — the block is the thing I kept wishing someone would fix, so I started working on it.

I'd like local and regional growers to be the first partners, and I'd rather build what they actually need than guess at it.
