The problem

Growing gourmet mushrooms is mostly not about growing mushrooms. It's about substrate.

Mixing, cooking, cooling, inoculating, incubating: hours of labor and a large share of your floor space, spent before a single mushroom starts to form. It's also the part that scales worst. Every block you want to fruit costs you more prep time and more room to do the prep in, so the ceiling on the operation ends up being set by the least interesting part of it.

Which is why, at some point, most growers work out that it's worth paying someone else to make the block.

What we're building

A ready-to-fruit block you buy instead of make. Prep hours go back into growing and selling. Prep space becomes fruiting space.

We're not selling yet: no orders, no waitlist, no pre-orders. Right now I'm talking to growers, and the growers I talk to now are the ones I'd want as first trial partners.

How it works

Three things at once.

Scale. Making blocks a thousand at a time costs less per block than making them a hundred at a time. Most of the industry's substrate is made in small batches on-farm, which is the expensive way to do it.

Custom machinery. Several steps in substrate prep have no good equipment behind them, so they get done by hand. We're building the machines for those steps.

A patent-pending method that takes a large part of the prep process out altogether. That's the piece I can't say more about until the patent work is further along, and I'd rather say that plainly than talk around it.

Where this came from

About five years of testing sits behind this, and it didn't start with mushrooms. It started with a bioreactor.

Aerated liquid culture in a stirred glass vessel: growth parameters, response to different nutrients, flocculation control. Learning what the organism actually wants, measured rather than guessed.

Before mushrooms, my background was contamination control in aerospace. Keeping a process clean at industrial scale is the same problem in both places, and it's the through-line here.
