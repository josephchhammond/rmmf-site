The problem

Substrate is the wall — and it's a space problem before it's anything else.

The prep line, the clean room, the incubation racks: they take the square footage you'd rather be fruiting in, and they cap how much you can grow no matter how much you can sell. Then there's what you lose. A contaminated block doesn't cost you the few dollars of sawdust in it — it costs you the $20–30 of mushrooms it would have grown.

Most growers know the trade already: at some point it becomes worth paying someone else to make the block, just to get the room and the hours back.

What we're building

A ready-to-fruit block that resists contamination on its own — and a reworked way of making it. Prep space becomes growing space.

We're not selling yet. No orders, no waitlist, no pre-orders. Right now I'm talking to growers to make sure the thing we build is the thing that's actually worth switching for — and the growers I talk to now are the ones I'd want as first trial partners.

Questions growers ask

How does it work?
The short answer is that it's a change to the block itself rather than to your process — which is the part I can't say more about until the patent work is further along. I'd rather be straight about that than hand-wave.
What will it cost?
I won't quote a price I can't yet stand behind. The target is to compete on what a block loses you, not just what it costs you.
When can I get one?
Not this year. The bench work has to finish first, then trials. If you want to be in the first round, the survey below is how you get on that list.
Are you real?
A Colorado LLC formed in January 2025, one person, a filed provisional, and a bench campaign running now. Small and real beats big and vapor.
